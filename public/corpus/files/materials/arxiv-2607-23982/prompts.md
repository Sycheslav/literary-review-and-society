### A.7 Counterfactual Credit and Prompt Optimization

Counterfactual multi-agent methods estimate an agent’s marginal contribution by replacing its action while holding other behavior fixed, as in difference rewards and COMA, while causal-influence rewards compare how alternative actions change another agent’s behavior ([Tumer et al., 2002](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#bib.bib38); [Foerster et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#bib.bib8); [Jaques et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#bib.bib15)). Robust control studies worst-case objectives over uncertainty sets, and GEPA proposes natural-language prompt revisions through reflection ([Iyengar, 2005](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#bib.bib14); [Agrawal et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#bib.bib1)). CREDIT defines a different optimization problem and update operator. For every visible template, it creates identically observed hidden-state worlds, replays the complete acquisition–communication–use policy under query and no-query alternatives, computes opportunity-cost-adjusted robust causal credit, and applies stage-dependent feasibility and transition rules to select the next executable policy. This operator determines the optimizer’s evidence, credit, candidate order, and state transition; GEPA functions only as its replaceable candidate-text generator. The empirical comparison with standard GEPA holds that proposal backend and the held-out game fixed, while mapping interventions, replay diagnostics, and stage-level metrics expose the consequences of CREDIT’s distinct update rule.

## Appendix H Frozen-Prompt Information-Structure Interventions

The frozen-prompt information-structure interventions underlying Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.3 In-Context Learning Changes the Effective Information Structure ‣ 3 Results ‣ Moral Hazard in Multi-Agent Language Models") center on Qwen3-4B IT, OLMo-7B, and Gemma 4B; Subsection [H.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#A8.SS6 "H.6 Additional Frozen-Prompt Models ‣ Appendix H Frozen-Prompt Information-Structure Interventions ‣ Moral Hazard in Multi-Agent Language Models") evaluates Command R7B, Granite 3.3-8B, OpenThinker3-7B, Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3.5-9B, and SmolLM3-3B under the same intervention. Three independently optimized GEPA programs per model are frozen before evaluation. Every frozen program is evaluated without further optimization under three rank–label mappings: *original* preserves the optimization-time relationship between public utility rank and the hidden unsafe label, *balanced* decorrelates that relationship, and *reversed* inverts it. All nine matched open-weight models use held-out evaluations with visible templates and deterministic temperature-1 sampling streams paired across policies and mappings. Balanced and reversed performance is never used for prompt optimization or selection. Muse Spark 1.1’s existing three-replicate primary evaluation supplies the Original Base reference, while canonical base policies are evaluated on matched visible templates under balanced and reversed mappings. Claude Opus 4.8’s existing three-replicate primary evaluation supplies Original Base, and three matched unoptimized policies are evaluated under balanced and reversed mappings. Gemma-2-2B IT contributes a separate frozen-policy analysis. The nine-model hierarchical contrasts and the additional model profiles use their corresponding sample structures.

The three independently optimized prompts produce exactly the same team-success trajectory: $100.0\%$ under the original relationship, $12.5\%$ under balanced assignment, and $0.0\%$ under reversal. Corresponding prompt-level declines of $87.5$ and $100.0$ percentage points are therefore common to all three programs rather than driven by one optimization seed. Query use and realized transfer remain zero under both shifted mappings, and format validity remains $100\%$ throughout. The intervention thus identifies a change in decision content rather than a formatting failure or re-entry into the designated query pathway.

The matched shifted controls sharpen that identification. Under balanced assignment, the canonical base policy reaches $89.1\%$ team success with $86.7\%$ query and transfer rates, compared with $12.5\%$ team success and no querying for every frozen prompt. Under reversal, the base reaches $100.0\%$ team success with $85.2\%$ query and transfer rates, while every frozen prompt reaches $0.0\%$ team success without querying. The shifted environments therefore remain solvable through direct revelation; performance changes because the optimized no-query rule loses its predictive relationship.

The reversed condition further localizes the learned rule. Unsafe choice is $0.0\%$ even though final accuracy and team success are $0.0\%$. The prompts continue to treat the highest-public-utility action as unsafe after the reversal makes that action safe, and therefore choose the second-highest-utility action. Their outputs remain valid and avoid the hidden unsafe action, but no longer select the target highest-utility safe action. This combination directly identifies the optimization-time utility rank as the predictive signal used by the frozen policy. The programs themselves are reproduced in Appendix [N](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23982v7#A14 "Appendix N GEPA-Optimized Prompts and Learned Information Policies ‣ Moral Hazard in Multi-Agent Language Models").
